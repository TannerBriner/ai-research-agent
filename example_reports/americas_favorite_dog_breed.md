# America's Favorite Dog Breed

## The French Bulldog's Reign

The French Bulldog is currently America's favorite dog breed, according to the American Kennel Club (AKC), which uses purebred dog registration statistics to determine popularity rankings. The French Bulldog has maintained the No. 1 spot for four consecutive years, from 2022 through 2025 (AKC) [1].

This represents a historic shift in American dog preferences. The Labrador Retriever previously held the title of America's most popular breed for an unprecedented 31 years before being dethroned in 2022 (AKC) [2]. The French Bulldog's ascent has been dramatic—the breed ranked as the 38th most popular dog in 2005 and rose to No. 9 by 2014, before claiming the top spot just a few years later (KMBC) [3].

## The Top 10 Most Popular Breeds

Based on AKC registration statistics for 2025, the complete top 10 ranking is as follows (AKC) [1], (PetMD) [4]:

Notable changes in 2025 include the Dachshund entering the top five for the first time, displacing the Poodle (PetMD) [4].

## Why French Bulldogs Became #1

Multiple factors have driven the French Bulldog's rise to the top of American dog preferences. Most significantly, their size and adaptability make them ideal for apartment living and urban environments. As more Americans live in smaller urban spaces, the French Bulldog's compact stature becomes increasingly appealing compared to larger, more active breeds (ABC News) [5]. According to an AKC spokesperson, "as we do smaller living, and more people live in apartments, they're highly adaptable. They're good in small spaces" (ABC News) [5].

Beyond housing considerations, French Bulldogs appeal to families because of their temperament. The breed is known for being affectionate and family-friendly, with low exercise requirements compared to traditional family dogs like Labradors (ABC News) [5]. They are described as having a strong desire for companionship, making them suitable for various lifestyles (ABC News) [5].

## Important Data Caveat: AKC vs. Broader Ownership

It is important to note that the AKC rankings measure registered purebred dogs only, which represents a significant limitation. Most dogs in the United States are mixed-breed dogs that are not registered with the AKC. Alternative data sources, such as pet insurance records, show different results. For instance, U.S. News & World Report's analysis of more than 1.2 million dog insurance records collected between 2022 and 2025 found that the Labrador Retriever is the most popular dog breed nationwide (U.S. News) [6]. This discrepancy suggests that while French Bulldogs dominate AKC registrations among purebred owners, Labrador Retrievers remain the most common dog across the broader U.S. dog-owning population.

Additionally, regional preferences vary significantly. Labrador Retrievers remain the most popular breed in 16 states, and no single breed dominates nationwide when looking at insurance and shelter data (Visual Capitalist) [7].

## Health Concerns and Breeding Implications

The French Bulldog's surging popularity has raised significant concerns among animal welfare advocates. The breed is brachycephalic, meaning it has a flattened face structure that can lead to serious health issues, including breathing difficulties and brachycephalic obstructive airway syndrome (BOAS) (Steve Dale) [8]. The ASPCA has warned that the breed's popularity has increased their prevalence in puppy mills, where dogs are bred in poor conditions with little regard for health or welfare (ASPCA) [9].

## Summary

The French Bulldog has become America's favorite purebred dog breed according to AKC registration statistics, holding the No. 1 position for four consecutive years and ending the Labrador Retriever's remarkable 31-year reign. This shift reflects changing American lifestyles, particularly the rise of urban apartment living and preference for smaller, lower-energy companions. However, broader ownership data suggests the Labrador Retriever remains more common across the entire U.S. dog population, highlighting the difference between purebred registration trends and actual pet ownership patterns.
